# Outreach Compliance Policy (UK GDPR + PECR)

Last reviewed: 2026-11-15. Not legal advice — George should get a one-off check from a solicitor or the ICO helpline if unsure.

Sources:
- ICO, Business-to-business marketing: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/
- ICO, Rules on direct marketing by electronic mail: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/guide-to-pecr/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/what-are-the-rules-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail
- Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 — PECR fines now aligned with UK GDPR (up to £17.5m / 4% turnover). Treat PECR breaches as serious.

## 1. Who we can email without consent

PECR's email-marketing rule (reg. 22) protects **individual subscribers**. It does not apply to **corporate subscribers**.

| Recipient type | PECR status | Cold email allowed? |
|---|---|---|
| Limited company (Ltd/PLC) | Corporate | Yes, with identification + opt-out |
| LLP | Corporate | Yes, with identification + opt-out |
| Scottish partnership | Corporate | Yes, with identification + opt-out |
| Sole trader | Individual | **No** — needs consent (soft opt-in doesn't apply to cold prospects) |
| Partnership in England, Wales or NI (non-LLP) | Individual | **No** — needs consent |
| Unknown / can't verify | Treat as individual | **No** |

Verification = the business's legal entity found on Companies House (number recorded) **and** a link between that company and the website/trading name (company name or number on the site, or matching registered/trading address). Record both in the CRM.

Reality check: many cafés, barbers, cleaners and tradespeople are sole traders. Expect a large share of discovered businesses in those sectors to fail this check. That is the correct result — don't stretch it.

## 2. Which address

- Use generic role addresses on the business's own domain (`info@`, `enquiries@`, `hello@`, `office@`) published on its own site.
- Named-person addresses (`jane@…`) are personal data. Use only if no role address exists, and record a short legitimate-interests note (purpose: offering website services relevant to their business; necessity: no generic address; balance: business-context address, one message, easy opt-out).
- Webmail addresses (gmail, outlook, hotmail, btinternet, etc.) → do not contact. They usually indicate a sole trader or a personal account.
- Never guess or pattern-build addresses.

## 3. Every message must

- Identify George Meyer and Formivane Digital truthfully.
- Give a working reply address for opt-out and say plainly how to opt out.
- Link to a Formivane privacy notice (UK GDPR Art. 14 transparency). **Must be on formivanedigital.co.uk**, not a third-party page.
- Carry a truthful, non-deceptive subject line.

## 4. Lawful basis record (per lead)

Record in CRM "Compliance basis":
- `Corporate subscriber – PECR reg 22 n/a; UK GDPR legitimate interests` — cleared
- `Individual subscriber – blocked` — do not email; add to suppression list with reason "Sole trader/partnership"
- `Unverified` — do not email until resolved

## 5. Follow-ups

Maximum one follow-up, no earlier than 7 days after first send, only if no reply/opt-out/bounce, and only with George's approval of the specific text. No automated sequences.

## 6. Objections

Any reply that objects, in any wording ("no thanks", "remove me", "not interested, don't email") → suppress immediately, no further contact, no "sorry to see you go" reply unless George asks.
